The four stages of managing a disaster

Disaster management is a cycle. Digital tools now support every stage.

1. Predict

Weather satellites, radar, river gauges and seismic networks collect data. Computer models turn it into forecasts for cyclones, floods and heavy rain. Earthquakes cannot yet be predicted, but networks can detect them within seconds.

2. Warn

Forecasts only help if people hear them. Warnings travel by SMS, cell broadcast, radio, television, sirens and apps, in local languages.

3. Respond

Drones, satellite images and GIS maps show which areas are damaged and which roads are open. Control rooms use this to send rescue teams and relief where it is needed first.

4. Recover

Damage data supports faster assessment and relief payments. The lessons from each event improve the next forecast, map and evacuation plan.

Technologies that make the difference

Satellites and remote sensing

Track cyclones, monitor floods and map damage over large areas, day and night.

IoT sensors

River-level, rainfall and soil-moisture sensors send live readings to flood and landslide warning systems.

Artificial intelligence

Machine learning improves rainfall and flood forecasts and can sort damage in satellite images much faster than people can.

GIS and mapping

Layers of roads, shelters, hospitals and flood zones help planners pick safe evacuation routes.

Mobile alerts

SMS, cell broadcast and apps reach people directly. India uses the Common Alerting Protocol to send standard alerts across channels.

Drones

Reach cut-off areas to survey damage, find survivors and drop supplies.

India: what early warning achieved

1999 → 2019

Odisha’s Super Cyclone of 1999 killed around 10,000 people. When Cyclone Fani hit in 2019, about 1.2 million people were evacuated in advance, and deaths were a small fraction of that earlier toll.

What changed

Better forecasts from the India Meteorological Department, clear and repeated warnings, cyclone shelters and trained local volunteers worked together. Technology helped, but only because people acted on it.

After the 2004 Indian Ocean tsunami, India also built a dedicated tsunami warning centre at INCOIS, Hyderabad.
